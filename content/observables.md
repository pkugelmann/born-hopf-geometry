# What would count as a physical explanation?

A geometric pattern may be beautiful and exact without explaining nature. The physical test is sharper: **which measured quantity follows from the geometry, by what operator and action, and with which independent checks?** Born–Hopf Geometry is organized to work toward answers to that question.

## Particle identity and mass

A particle is not established just because a representation has the correct dimension or a geometric coefficient resembles a known mass. The framework needs a *physical excitation*: a mode supported by an actual source, visible as an appropriate pole or spectral response, with its mass fixed in a common calibrated scheme.

The finite charge and matter constructions are developed in [Paper V-A](paper.html?paper=Paper5A) and the spectrum programme in [Paper V-B](paper.html?paper=Paper5B). Some structural roles and finite numerical relations are derived; identifying the interacting physical poles remains conditional on source and matching certificates.

## Why particles decay—and which channels dominate

The natural observable is not merely an allowed arrow from one particle label to another. It is a **partial width**: the rate of a particular decay channel, which determines branching fractions and lifetimes when all open channels are accounted for.

For Born–Hopf Geometry, a proposed geometric decay network must supply actual transition operators, enforce the appropriate conservation and selection rules, specify direction through the relevant energy and state structure, and determine relative amplitudes. A network based on the 600-cell or a Clifford hierarchy is a promising *candidate structure*, but adjacency alone cannot substitute for a source-owned transition amplitude and a phase-space calculation.

The existing papers offer finite carrier, interaction and hadronic ingredients; **a comprehensive parameter-free decay theory is not certified by the current corpus**. [Matter and interactions](papers.html?volume=V) · [Hadronic readouts](paper.html?paper=Paper7B).

## Strength and structure of interactions

The observable questions include which charges exist, why a current couples, the strengths and signs of interactions, how different channels mix, and what scattering or bound-state responses a detector sees.

A symmetry group describes possible transformations. To explain a measured interaction, the framework must also derive the right common action, current, normalization, external-source response and measurable matrix elements. [Paper V-C](paper.html?paper=Paper5C) addresses these distinctions; [Paper IV-B](paper.html?paper=Paper4B) develops quantum response.

## Gravitational coupling and cosmological history

The gravitational test is not simply whether a curvature expression can be written down. It is whether one native action yields the correct weak-field response and the observed gravitational coupling, with the same normalization used elsewhere. The cosmological extension must then account for expansion, perturbations, structure growth and other astronomical data.

[Paper VI-A](paper.html?paper=Paper6A) uses a specified working action and studies its geometric consequences. The latest source release keeps the **microscopic action identification and physical Newton-coupling match open**. [Paper VI-B](paper.html?paper=Paper6B) contains observational pushforwards on their declared branches and source conditions.

## Hadrons, confinement and quantum fields

The strong-interaction challenge concerns measured hadron masses, binding energies, form factors, distributions, thermal behavior, and scattering—not only the existence of color algebra. The framework's finite Hurwitz structures and source operators provide a mathematical starting point, while the physical predictions require relevant states, dynamics and analytic limits.

[Paper VII](papers.html?volume=VII) develops these layers, including a conditional continuum limit with a fixed ultraviolet momentum bound. The finite geometry does **not**, by itself, certify a uniform Yang–Mills mass gap, an asymptotic string tension or an entire table of observed hadronic quantities.

## New transition results: what they do and do not determine

The most recent [Paper VII–B](paper.html?paper=Paper7B) source contains an exact symmetry chart for decuplet–octet axial comparisons. It also states conditional restrictions on threshold and P-wave channels and makes explicit why a scalar overlap or mass hierarchy alone cannot determine the full physical transition strength. The geometric structure is more restrictive than an arbitrary channel-by-channel weight table, but **observed branching ratios and lifetimes still require a dynamically selected current and open-channel calculation**.

## What would make a result especially convincing?

A strong result would derive an observable **without choosing new channel-specific weights or importing the measured answer**, state all mathematical conditions, and yield a prediction that can be checked on independent data. One successful number is less persuasive than a consistent family of related results—especially if the same source, action and normalization work across multiple sectors.
