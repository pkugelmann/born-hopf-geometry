# A route through the geometry

The papers are not best read as 31 isolated PDFs. They form a dependency programme. These routes offer an entry point based on the question you want to answer. Each route moves from **the relevant geometric object** toward **the observable it is supposed to explain**.

## 1. Why a three-sphere?

Begin with the compact three-sphere, its symmetries, and the Hopf fibration. The important distinction is between a **mathematically available structure** and a **physically selected carrier**. [Paper I–A](paper.html?paper=Paper1A) develops the mathematical geometry; [Paper I–B](paper.html?paper=Paper1B) introduces exceptional finite symmetries; [Paper II–A](paper.html?paper=Paper2A) states the constitutional principles; [Paper II–B](paper.html?paper=Paper2B) handles the momentum-carrier selection.

**Question to track:** which finite structure is selected and under which exact hypotheses?

## 2. Why position and momentum belong together

The paired eight-dimensional **M₈** construction is not eight everyday spatial dimensions. It is a reciprocal geometric setting for localization, phases and transport. Read [Paper I–C](paper.html?paper=Paper1C), then [Paper II–C](paper.html?paper=Paper2C) and [Paper II–D](paper.html?paper=Paper2D).

**Question to track:** which measurement limitations and observable kinematics follow from allowed operators, rather than an imposed measurement model?

## 3. How can geometry become dynamics?

An operator spectrum is not a history. A physical explanation needs an action, a source, evolution and observable readout. Read [Paper III–A](paper.html?paper=Paper3A), [Paper III–B](paper.html?paper=Paper3B), [Paper III–F](paper.html?paper=Paper3F), then [Paper IV–A](paper.html?paper=Paper4A).

**Question to track:** what is derived for a declared action family, and what establishes which action actually governs a measured process?

## 4. Where do particles and their properties enter?

The matter sequence asks how charge, chirality, gauge roles, family structure and masses arise. Read [Paper V–A](paper.html?paper=Paper5A), [Paper V–B](paper.html?paper=Paper5B), and [Paper V–C](paper.html?paper=Paper5C). Pair these with [Paper IV–B](paper.html?paper=Paper4B) when a propagating physical pole is the target.

**Question to track:** which geometric source is the physically occurring one, and how would it produce a measurable pole or mass?

## 5. What decides decay channels and lifetimes?

The five-core, 600-cell, Clifford and Hurwitz constructions provide finite transition structures. Read [Paper I–B](paper.html?paper=Paper1B), [Paper VII–A](paper.html?paper=Paper7A), and [Paper VII–B](paper.html?paper=Paper7B). The latest source adds conditional axial comparison strengths, but warns against treating mass differences or scalar overlap as full decay couplings.

**Question to track:** what selects the source-owned current and open-channel response from which a *partial width* would follow?

## 6. What about gravity and hadronic physics?

For gravitational coupling and cosmology, follow [Paper III–E](paper.html?paper=Paper3E), [Paper VI–A](paper.html?paper=Paper6A), and [Paper VI–B](paper.html?paper=Paper6B). For strong-interaction operators and the continuum limit, use [Papers VII–A to VII–C](papers.html?volume=VII).

**Question to track:** can one common normalized action reach independent gravitational, cosmological or hadronic measurements without adjustable sector-specific input?
