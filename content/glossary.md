# A short guide to the vocabulary

These definitions are intended to make the research readable without replacing the precise mathematical definitions in the manuscripts.

## Three-sphere (S³)

A compact three-dimensional space, expressible as the set of unit vectors in four dimensions or as the unit quaternions. It is not the ordinary two-dimensional spherical surface we see in three-dimensional space.

## SU(2) and Spin(4)

SU(2) is a group of transformations closely tied to unit quaternions and S³. Spin(4) has two SU(2) factors and encodes the spin-symmetry structure of the round three-sphere. They help classify representation spaces and operators.

## Hopf fibration

A structured map from S³ to S² with circular fibres. Its linking and topology provide invariants and constraints, not a literal image of particles moving along a set of wires.

## 24-cell and 600-cell

Highly symmetric regular four-dimensional polytopes. The 24-cell has 24 vertices and the 600-cell has 120 vertices; their finite quaternionic symmetries help organize the framework's exceptional geometry.

## Clifford algebra

An algebra designed to represent geometric products and spinorial structures. The framework uses Clifford-related grading and hierarchy to organize finite carriers and candidate dynamical terms.

## Reciprocal geometry (M₈)

An eight-dimensional product with paired position-like and momentum-like roles, each containing an SU(2) factor and a U(1) circle factor. The eight-dimensional description concerns geometric and operator structure, not eight ordinary visible spatial coordinates.

## Representation / carrier

A representation describes how a symmetry acts on a space of mathematical states. A carrier is the space on which these operations act. Its mathematical availability does not, by itself, establish physical occurrence.

## Action and source

An action is a functional whose variation supplies equations of motion and response relationships. A source is what couples to or excites a state or current. The manuscripts keep track of whether these are actually derived and realized, rather than merely postulated as possible.

## Spectral pole

A distinctive mathematical feature of an operator's resolvent or correlation response. Under the right physical conditions it can identify an excitation, but a formal pole is not automatically a measured particle.

## Observable / readout

A quantity whose operational definition connects a model to a measurement—such as a mass, lifetime, cross section, gravitational response or cosmological spectrum. The connecting map needs units, state preparation, calibration and an appropriate source/detector description.

## Conditional theorem

A mathematical result that is established when specified hypotheses hold. It is not a failed theorem; the crucial question is whether the required hypotheses are derived and physically instantiated in the framework.

## Proof-chain DAG

A directed acyclic graph representing dependencies among definitions, constructions, theorems and unresolved certificates. It is a way to track what an observable depends on and where new evidence is genuinely needed.
